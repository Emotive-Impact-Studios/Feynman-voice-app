1 · Bring your topic

Start with your own notes.

Add lecture slides, a PDF or a photo of your page. Or just say the topic. Fenny reads what you give it so it can check you against your course.

2 · Teach Fenny

Talk it through, like it's never heard of it.

No typing and no flashcards. You explain, and Fenny cuts in with the naive questions a clever first year would ask.

3 · Hit a wall

Where you stall is a gap.

That pause when you can't say why is the most useful moment of revision. Fenny notes it quietly and lets you carry on.

4 · See what you missed

Every gap, in plain words.

After each session you see what you explained clearly and what you skipped, fudged or got wrong. Each gap becomes a card for later.

Questions

Good ones, too.

Will Fenny explain things I don't understand?

No. Fenny is your student, not your tutor. When you get stuck it asks a smaller question to help you find your way, and the gap goes on your list so you can look it up in your own notes.

Do I have to upload notes?

No. You can just say a topic and start talking. Notes help Fenny check you against your course, so the gaps it finds are the ones your exam cares about.

What subjects does it work for?

Anything you can explain out loud: sciences, law, medicine, economics, history, philosophy. If it's on a slide, you can teach it.

Do I have to talk out loud in the library?

Quietly is fine. Fenny listens closely, so a low voice with earphones works. Many students use it on a walk.

When can I use it?

We're testing it with a small group of students now. iPhone comes first, Android after. Join the waitlist and we'll let you know when it's your turn.
